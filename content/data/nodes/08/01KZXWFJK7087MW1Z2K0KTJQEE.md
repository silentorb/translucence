# Description

A high ground fallacy occurs when someone challenges the validity of a worldview due to it containing a certain problem when the world view of the challenger contains that same problem.

> 💡In this context the problem is a seeming contradiction within a worldview that must be resolved as either a [[01KZXWFJGHQXTGH942SMX0VG56]] or a logical flaw.

For someone to employ such a fallacy, he must generally be unaware that his own worldview contains the same problem.

# Double irony

Sometimes when a high ground fallacy is employed, the problem has a reasonable solution within the challenged worldview and thus is only a paradox, while the problem has no solution in the challenger's worldview.  In such cases, the challenger is using sleight-of-hand to wield the flaws of his own worldview as weapons.

# Similar valid cases

A high ground fallacy must be distinguished from the similar case of someone requiring someone else to account for a mutually shared and acknowledged worldview problem.

Such a case is not contradictory on the challenger's part, because the challenger is not suggesting that his own worldview does not possess that problem, nor that a worldview is automatically disproven by its presence.
